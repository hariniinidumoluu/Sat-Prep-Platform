Command of Evidence: Experimental Findings
Hard
Researchers studying moth navigation proposed that some moths do not simply fly toward the brightest object at night. Instead, the insects may use the pattern of polarized light in the sky to maintain a heading, with artificial lights disrupting that cue. To distinguish the two explanations, the researchers tested moths in two enclosed arenas with equal overall brightness. In one arena the polarization pattern stayed fixed; in the other it rotated slowly, though the intensity of light remained unchanged. Which finding would most directly support the researchers' proposed explanation?
Moths in both arenas spent more time near the light source than in unlit holding containers.
Moths in the rotating-pattern arena maintained straighter paths than moths in the fixed-pattern arena.
Moths in the fixed-pattern arena maintained consistent headings, whereas those in the rotating-pattern arena repeatedly changed direction.
Moths in both arenas flew farther when the light sources were moved closer to the enclosure walls.
C
Only the polarization pattern changed between the equally bright arenas. More stable headings with fixed polarization, and disrupted headings when it rotated, would favor a navigation-cue explanation over attraction to brightness alone.
